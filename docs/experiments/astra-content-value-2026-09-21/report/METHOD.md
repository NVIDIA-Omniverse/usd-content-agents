# Matched Astra / Content Agents development pilot

This experiment asks whether the public Content Agents workflows improve completion of bounded physical asset tasks over direct Astra authoring. It is a ten-pair development-set pilot. It is not a sealed benchmark, a statistical estimate of production performance, or a real-hardware validation.

## Experimental unit and intervention

Each of ten independently sourced online assets has two fresh authoring contexts. Both use `gpt-6-astra`, `ultra` reasoning, the same original source closure, task contract, low-level authoring/render/physics tools, assigned host and GPU, and a 2,400-second wall-time limit. Child-model work is included in that time. Two repair iterations after the first candidate are allowed by instruction and audited from retained chronology. The watchdog enforces wall time; it does not mechanically identify repairs. There is no token cap: token usage is an outcome.

The treatment arm reads and uses the pinned repository's public Content Agents workflow skills, orchestration, handoffs and validation contracts. It must retain failed or conditional dispositions. The control author directly uses the common low-level tools and may write task-specific scripts, but may not read or invoke Content Agents workflows or decision helpers. Both may delegate to additional Astra Ultra workers within their total budget. The experiment therefore compares workflows, not a fixed number of model calls.

Odd-numbered cases run control first; even-numbered cases run treatment first. This deterministic balancing is not randomization. There is one authoring attempt per arm and asset, with five downstream simulation seeds. The seeds are repeated physical trials of one authored asset, not five independent authoring experiments.

## Source and evaluator independence

Ten source-curation agents acquired original online design/visualization assets, preserving existing CAD dependencies, annotations, defects and visual animations. Physics was not stripped from ready simulation models to manufacture inputs. The scored source closure contains 486 files, with exact hashes and pinned download URLs. Additional curation documentation is outside an arm's input unless included in its frozen closure.

The global protocol froze before scored authoring. Each task, evaluator snapshot and source reference froze before that case's first arm. Evaluator authors remained blind to the scored submissions until their assigned contracts were frozen. The public task criteria and source inventory were available equally to both arms; private evaluator implementations and other arms were forbidden inputs. This is instruction-based separation, not an operating-system security boundary. Access and mutation audits supplement the instruction.

The evaluator reads the submitted USD and bindings, verifies original visible geometry and relevant component identity, derives actual USD bodies/joints/frames, and runs fresh native OvPhysX trajectories. It does not trust agent-authored success JSON, saved animation or render appearance. Evaluator-owned positive and negative fixtures qualify the source checks, physical controller and observers. Passing synthetic fixtures is a prerequisite for evaluation, not evidence that a submitted online asset passed.

Each case uses five fixed seeds: 11, 23, 47, 83 and 131. All required checks across all seeds must pass. Source fidelity, authored physical structure, physical motion, stability and applicable contact/retention gates are conjunctive. Scope varies by case: the vise test does not prove workpiece clamping; the engine does not simulate steam; the conveyor uses a declared rotary-annulus abstraction; the printer does not prove printing; the robot and hand tests do not establish hardware actuator or transmission realism. Excavator source fidelity is bounded to its exact converter output, whose completeness against the native CAD assembly was not certified.

## Outcomes and accounting

- **Task acceptance:** independent acceptance of the complete frozen task. Missing/null final deliverables are contractual rejections. Unsupported evaluator capabilities or unavailable solver evidence are inconclusive.
- **False pass:** an explicit author `claimed_accepted=true` followed by at least one concrete independent rejection. An inconclusive evaluator alone cannot establish a false pass. An author that correctly declines acceptance is not a false pass.
- **Human effort:** count observed human interventions during the autonomous authoring run. Human review time was not measured and remains null. Zero in-run interventions does not prove human labor savings. Automated curation, evaluator engineering, experiment orchestration and later review are separate preparation work.
- **Time:** measured authoring elapsed time, including unsuccessful attempts and permitted repairs. Time per accepted asset divides all arm authoring time by independently accepted assets. With zero accepted assets, the ratio is undefined.
- **Usage:** reconcile per-response client token records with the final cumulative counter of every distinct parent and child session. Do not count a parent counter again as a child, and do not substitute the primary turn's usage for the whole run. Missing final records or interrupted sessions make usage incomplete. This telemetry is not a provider invoice.
- **Cost:** uncached input, cached input, output and allocated GPU hours require explicit unit rates and rate provenance. Unknown rates remain null. No subscription is treated as free computation. Cost per accepted asset includes failed authoring attempts, excludes separately reported setup/evaluation, and is undefined when nothing is accepted.

Allocated GPU seconds are the assigned worker's elapsed time, not GPU-busy seconds. GPU utilization and power samples are retained separately. Reasoning tokens are a reported subset of output tokens and must not be billed a second time.

In the structured output, `independently_passing_assets` is the physical task count. The legacy `accepted` field additionally requires a valid individual author audit; it does not certify the global protocol. Only `matched_pair_eligible_accepted` applies both pair and global eligibility. A harness-owned timeout is marked `frozen_evaluator_status=NO_VERDICT` and `independent_task_status=INCONCLUSIVE`.

## Environment and limitations

The authoring environment comprises four full NVIDIA L40 GPUs with 48 GB each across two Horde nodes, two GPUs per node. Every pair remains on the same host and GPU. Each node has an eight-CPU quota and 64 GB RAM. GPU UUID isolation was verified with four simultaneous, visible OVRTX renders. Physics qualification and independent acceptance use the actual native OvPhysX integration; the GPU allocation does not imply that every operation uses GPU acceleration.

Public repository commit: `a96faf9cb2f5c1f655fe0d60c0ccf57e3477b1aa`. Both arms use Codex 0.154.0 because the repository's bundled 0.147.0 executable rejected the requested model. The repository's tracked source files were kept unchanged. Shared tools include OvPhysX 0.4.13, OVRTX 0.4.1.364340, OpenUSD 25.5, usd-convert-cad 0.2.0 and OCP 7.8.1.1. Python patch versions differ between nodes; each paired comparison remains on one node.

Evaluator qualification and some source preparation overlapped early scored authoring on shared CPUs. Later preparation/evaluation jobs used nice 15 and idle I/O scheduling. GPU isolation does not eliminate CPU, filesystem, network, provider-cache or service-load variation. Consequently elapsed-time differences are descriptive pilot measurements, not a clean causal latency estimate.

The frozen runtime did not provision every documented repair route. Scene Optimizer build resources are documented setup for optimized workflows, and the conveyor repair actually selected its unavailable de-instancing worker. Geogram requires a separately installed and digest-qualified Vorpalite worker, which was also unavailable. Both arms had the same installed low-level tools, but these omissions can disproportionately block a workflow that requires those routes. Hardware/render/physics qualification is not complete workflow-dependency qualification. These are outcomes of this configured installation; they do not establish how a fully provisioned workflow would perform, and dependency-blocked failures must not be attributed solely to workflow design.

The compute checkout contained the verified pinned tracked files but lacked Git metadata. Native asset launches for the vise, engine and excavator rejected package-owned `usd-cli` provenance before launching their children. That is another installation limitation. A later capstone uses a separate checkout with genuine Git provenance; it does not repair or replace scored outcomes.

The instruction boundary also failed in observed process-listing outputs: broad `ps` commands exposed inline source code from another concurrent case. Affected author runs are marked protocol-invalid, and both members of an affected pair are excluded from the clean matched subset. Physical acceptance is still independently measured and reported separately. This separates a functioning saved asset from an uncontaminated authoring experiment. No hidden reasoning or encrypted collaboration messages are used in this review.

The frozen protocol additionally declares a maximum of four concurrent simulation processes. The harness limited author GPU lanes but did not mechanically enforce a global process semaphore across author children and evaluator preparation. A retained process table directly lists five hinge native workers at 22:28:04.786 UTC. Separate evidence establishes at least five overlapping native processes at 23:04:52 UTC: four conveyor workers and a vise worker whose lifetime is reconstructed from its saved timestamp and monotonic duration. The author prompts did not disclose the global process cap; this is a harness obligation. The [global audit](global_protocol_audit.json) links both forms of evidence. No pair is labeled fully protocol-eligible; this does not assert that every arm exceeded the cap. Physical results remain separate, and the configured-runtime measurements do not establish a clean causal workflow comparison.

After scoring began, an analytic witness exposed a numerical defect in Trimesh closest-point distance calculations on submillimeter triangles expressed in meters. Some identical source surfaces were rejected. Frozen v1 records are retained; a separately qualified `source_distance_v1_1` adjudication changes only the numerical length unit used for distance calculation, preserves thresholds, area/bounds predicates, source bytes and authored USD, and requires five complete native trials. Every affected case is checked, including original passes. Physics is reused only with unchanged input/configuration/result hashes; conveyor physics is rerun with the unchanged controller if v1 had skipped it. Both outcomes are published. A known numerical defect cannot establish an author false pass.

The direct engine requires a further conservative measurement review. Its remaining rejections concern an auxiliary dynamic guide body, although the public task did not clearly forbid auxiliary bodies, and the evaluator's freefall witness. A small independent fixture reproduced the witness's erroneous displacement when a distant free body shared a scene with an unrelated highly iterated constrained body; gravitational velocity remained correct. Moving only that diagnostic witness near the origin passed the unchanged thresholds. The raw v1 and v1.1 rejections remain available, but the later hash-bound review marks the task inconclusive and withdraws a false-pass finding. It does not grant acceptance, change the asset, or relax the frozen task.

A separate [static delivery audit](../performance_research/structural_delivery_v1/README.md) inspected the exact submitted Content Agents scenes for cases 07–10 while expensive source checks were still running. All four composed scenes contain zero rigid-body, joint and collider APIs, despite nonempty mandatory joint roles in their frozen public tasks. The auditor passed 15 positive/negative synthetic checks on each structural runtime. Its original observations, qualification, code and input hashes are retained separately. Aggregation permits only these four pinned, qualified observations to change PENDING, INCONCLUSIVE or FAIL to FAIL; it cannot grant or override PASS. The rule verifies task, submission, output manifest, scene/bindings and composed-layer digest bindings, plus unchanged frozen evaluator code. Public reproduction uses exact sanitized submissions/manifests and separately verified launch projections; it does not require redistribution of non-drawer USD. Frozen evaluator status remains a separate field. Source fidelity and native runtime remain explicitly NOT_EVALUATED by this supplementary audit: the rejection is missing mandatory physical content, not a measured simulation failure or completed source evaluation.

After the independent rejection evidence was available, the experiment owner stopped the 07 dispatcher, the incomplete CA07 frozen evaluation and the incomplete direct 07 corrected source-distance evaluation. The [targeted termination receipt](../evaluation_attempts/pilot-v1/07_robot_arm/independent_early_stop_20260922.json) records validated command lines, signals and the supporting evidence. It is a scheduling record, not a replacement acceptance verdict. The unfinished source/frozen evaluations remain distinguishable from the supplementary CA task failure; no source-completion result is inferred from stopping them.

Physical passes are bounded by declared model assumptions. The excavator author multiplied moving-body rotational inertias by 12 without changing mass or center of mass, explicitly disclosing an abstract effective-inertia model that cannot be realized by mass confined to the visible parts. A passing articulation test would not validate the original excavator's dynamics. The robot-arm source contract compares cached part geometry and instance coverage; the original CAD mating transforms are not independently certified by that contract. These qualifications accompany any reported acceptance.

Initial setup exceeded a 25 GiB ephemeral-storage quota and evicted a node; it was restored before scored execution. One independent refrigerator-evaluation launch preceded transfer of its frozen evaluator snapshot and exited without inspecting the asset. That infrastructure attempt was retained separately, and the unchanged evaluator was rerun after verified transfer. Neither incident is an authoring failure.

OvPhysX contact `read_force_matrix()` values in the qualified runtime are impulses in N·s despite force terminology in the API. Native mass/timestep fixtures established the units. The frozen drawer detector uses its original raw threshold unchanged; its erroneously named force trace field is an impulse, and presentation converts it using the timestep. Other contact evaluators normalized the value before their freeze. The drawer erratum accompanies its evidence.

All authoring results, including early stops and failures, belong in the ten-asset denominator. Any later workflow repair or demonstration must be labeled a separate unscored follow-up, with different code, effort and acceptance evidence.

## Publication boundary

The public bundle includes the frozen protocol and task briefs, original-source hashes and download manifest, evaluator code and qualification receipts, measured results, accounting logic, and selected verified physical evidence. It excludes credentials, private model reasoning, internal service addresses, and raw private session files. Third-party source assets retain their upstream licensing and attribution; source-download instructions are preferred to republishing CAD with unresolved license metadata.

## Drawer collision approximation boundary

At three checked positions, the original visible drawer floor is 4.50 mm above the accepted collision proxy. The frozen payload lower face starts 8.00 mm below that visible floor and 3.50 mm below the proxy; the recorded 5 mm penetration gate measures the proxy. Thus the source checks preserve visual geometry, while physical acceptance applies to the declared collision approximation. [Read-only source/proxy diagnostic](drawer/source_proxy_clearance.json). No scored asset, frozen spawn position or threshold was changed after this diagnostic. It is a limitation of the qualified task, not a retroactive pass or failure. The capstone follow-up retains its own separate code and measurements.

A future freeze should separately specify visual-source fidelity, collider/contact fidelity, valid non-intersecting task initialization, and allowable mass/inertia assumptions. Physically unrealizable effective inertias must be scored as an explicitly separate abstraction rather than treated as faithful hardware dynamics. These are proposed qualification changes, not rules retroactively applied to this pilot.

The printer independent evaluator reached its existing 5,400-second limit before any verdict or native trajectory was produced. A [bounded-attempt assessment](../evaluation_attempts/pilot-v1/09_printer/plain_astra/bounded_closeout_20260922/timeout_assessment.json) and explicit wrapper provenance record INCONCLUSIVE. The wrapper belongs to the harness; it is not represented as a verdict emitted by the frozen evaluator. Source fidelity remains incomplete and native physics remains not evaluated. The attempt was neither extended nor restarted.
